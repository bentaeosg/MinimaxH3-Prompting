# Dialogue Reliability Protocol — v1.5 (Production Reliability Edition)

**Purpose:** Mandatory supplementary production protocol for converting an approved storyboard and its original storyboard-generation prompt directly into a MiniMax H3 full-reference video prompt, with particular attention to exact dialogue, speaker identity, clean speech boundaries, audio-source isolation, full-screen storyboard reconstruction, and cinematic detail.

**Status:** Supersedes Dialogue Reliability Protocol v1.4. Works alongside Official Minimax Prompting Guidelines.txt, Minimax Positive Prompts.txt, and Stylistic Preferences.txt v1.1. It does not supersede official syntax or authorize redesign of an approved storyboard. The combined repair packages tested in November 2026 were reported successful by the user; no individual mechanism was isolated experimentally. These are reliability practices, not deterministic guarantees.

## 1. Mandatory references, order, and precedence

Before each relevant conversion, actually read the complete contents, in order, of:
1. Official Minimax Prompting Guidelines.txt
2. Minimax Positive Prompts.txt
3. Stylistic Preferences.txt
4. DialogueProtocol.txt (this file)

If a file cannot be read, identify it and do not claim compliance with its unverified contents. Official guidance controls valid syntax; stylistic preferences control production-wide constraints; this protocol controls dialogue and permitted shot-time optimization. The original screenplay/storyboard-generation prompt governs exact words, speaker identity, emotional intent, and story actions; the approved storyboard governs visual shot coverage; assigned character references govern facial identity and wardrobe. Report irreconcilable conflicts rather than silently choosing.

## 2. Inputs and release gate

Required: (a) actual approved storyboard image, (b) original storyboard-generation prompt and authoritative screenplay excerpt where needed, (c) approved total duration (maximum 15 seconds), (d) explicit reference-upload map, and (e) any authorized exceptions, such as actual remote speech or unusual audio. A Seedance prompt is NOT a prerequisite. Inspect the actual panels, not merely the written storyboard description. If essential information is missing, ask one targeted question or label the output PROVISIONAL and not release-ready. Never invent dialogue, duration, or a speaker-to-panel assignment from an image alone.

**Fixed upload order:** `<Picture 0>` storyboard; `<Picture 1>` first explicitly assigned character/asset; `<Picture 2>` second explicitly assigned character/asset; subsequent pictures as explicitly mapped. Never assume `<Subject N>` equals `<Picture N>`. Subject numbering begins at 1. The operator header must identify the clip, selected duration, and every reference in upload order.

## 3. Source authority and allowable optimization

Preserve the approved storyboard's shot count, order, framing, camera geometry, physical blocking, visible subjects, essential action, environment, lighting, and dramatic progression. Preserve exact script dialogue and speaker ownership. Do not invent camera angles, shots, props, gestures, characters, sound effects, or dialogue.

Without requesting approval for each ordinary adjustment, the converter MAY redistribute durations among existing shots while preserving total duration, move an **intact** scripted dialogue event to a different existing shot where the same correct speaker's articulating mouth is clearly visible, shorten nonessential preparatory action, or make a shot a silent reaction after its complete dialogue has been safely reassigned. Do not move individual words, change line order, reassign speakers, remove essential action, or turn a back-facing listener into a speaking face. If a valid schedule cannot be made, block release and ask for an approved storyboard change, duration change, or separate generation.

**Production restriction:** No off-screen speech or voiceover by default. Every authorized dialogue event must have its actual speaking character visibly speaking, with the correct face and mouth sufficiently readable. If the script explicitly requires remote or off-screen speech, flag the exception for approval; do not silently delete or invent it.

## 4. Private preflight: shot inventory and dialogue manifest

Before drafting, construct two internal working tables:

**Shot inventory:** panel index, frame geometry, camera angle and direction, foreground/background, visible people, mouth visibility, initial visual state, essential action, permitted camera movement, lighting sources, location, intended emotion, and available duration.

**Dialogue manifest:** one row per authorized vocal event: exact verbatim text and punctuation; speaking character; stable `<Subject N>`; global `(Sx)` assigned by first audible occurrence; shot/panel; estimated onset and natural completion; visible mouth; performance direction; previous and next event; intervening audio state; and whether any communication device or video display is present. Include explicit `SILENT` entries for all shots without speech and for meaningful pre-line, between-line, and post-line intervals.

The first human vocal sound must be the first word of the first authorized event. No pre-line mutter, throat-clear, synthetic syllable, unassigned background voice, or spoken notification. Natural nonvocal breathing is permitted only when consistent with the source.

## 5. Mandatory six-section output and detail standard

Produce exactly these six MiniMax sections in order:
`subject_definitions`, `summary`, `retention_analysis`, `detailed_description`, `overall_soundscape`, `non_diegetic_music`.

Use official reference-label, retention-marker, shot, dialogue, and speaker syntax. Use `[reference generation]` when the storyboard is a planning reference rather than a literal first-frame image. In `detailed_description`, use `[Shot 1]` for the opening and `[Shot N] At MM:SS.mmm, ...` for subsequent hard cuts. The user-selected total duration must be explicit in the operator header and summary; the last shot ends at that duration.

**High-detail standard:** A prompt must be complete, independently usable, and cinematically specific. Each shot should establish its full-frame composition, camera placement, correct character identity, posture and gaze, physical environment, light-source geography and facial-shadow direction, actions and emotional progression, authorized speech, natural sound, and cut. Preserve relevant detail from the storyboard-generation prompt. Do not replace shot direction with vague instructions such as 'follow the storyboard.' The official guide's normal generation-description length is a guide, not a ceiling; prioritize the information load of the scene. Do not pad with redundant prohibitions, repetitive plot summaries, or duplicate dialogue.

## 6. Single authoritative dialogue event rule

Each authorized scripted vocal event appears **exactly once** as one `<d>[Language] ...</d>` tag, exclusively in its assigned shot in `detailed_description`. Use the exact scripted words in chronological order. A complete two-sentence statement by one speaker may occupy one tag when it is one continuous speech event; a short natural pause inside it does not create another tag. Never repeat its quoted words in `summary`, `retention_analysis`, `overall_soundscape`, headings, sound instructions, or global restrictions. Do not create a second `<d>` tag to emphasize, echo, or describe the same line.

Example format (illustrative only, NOT dialogue to insert into unrelated clips):
`<Subject 2> (S1), visibly speaking, delivers one concise performance direction: <d>[English]We can't. The archive has been distributed.</d>`

The `<Subject N>` identifies the visible referenced character; `(Sx)` identifies the voice. Assign speaker IDs by first vocal occurrence, not by subject number; reuse them without renumbering across shots. Do not write speaker IDs in `retention_analysis`. English lines must be intelligible English; other scripted languages use their actual language labels. Never invent accents or phonetic syllables. Standardize only punctuation when official guidance requires it, without changing words.

## 7. Dialogue-first construction and visual speaker proof

Within each speaking shot: (1) establish the approved camera composition and correct speaker's face; (2) identify `<Subject N> (Sx)` and one concise, emotionally precise vocal direction; (3) place the single authorized `<d>` tag near the start of the shot's action description; (4) describe accurate synchronized mouth articulation; (5) specify natural vocal release, resting lips, and the storyboard-grounded silent follow-through. Keep detailed visual cinematography elsewhere in the same shot rather than burying the speech tag beneath long preambles.

At a speaker change, the hard cut and the new speaking face must be established **before** the next voice starts. Do not allow the prior speaker to continue over the next character's reaction shot. In a two-shot, identify the speaking person unambiguously and describe the other as a non-speaking listener. The presence of a headset, microphone, or nearby monitor does not alter speaker ownership.

## 8. Speech-window design: natural delivery and clean boundaries

Give every dialogue event a feasible onset and completion within its assigned shot. Begin promptly after the face is established; do not consume its available speech time with unnecessary looks, reaches, equipment operation, or lengthy setup. Use approximate times only where they clarify event ownership and boundaries; avoid forced phoneme-level micromanagement. Do not rush an emotionally important line to fit an impossible window.

**Post-line release:** After the final authorized word, the speaker closes or relaxes the mouth naturally and performs a concrete silent action (gaze, posture, hands, restrained reaction) already supported by the storyboard. The soundtrack then returns to the explicitly permitted environmental sound. For a short speaking shot, prefer a prompt hard cut soon after natural completion, rather than a long unassigned conversational gap. A meaningful silent reaction remains intact if it is part of the approved storyboard; do not erase it solely to shorten silence.

**Between events:** Every interval without authorized speech has a defined audio state: environmental sound only, or true silence if the source requires it. Distinguish a brief pause *within* one continuous sentence from a silent gap *between* separate vocal events. Do not suggest 'he begins to answer,' 'she searches for words,' 'an exchange continues,' or similar invitations to generate unscripted speech.

**Final-line lock:** Identify the last authorized vocal event and its natural completion. From that moment to the clip's end, only permitted environmental sound may remain. If the last shot is a silent reaction, its character has a resting mouth and no speech. The ending is not an invitation to repeat the final line.

## 9. Continuous statements and shot boundaries

Prefer to keep a complete sentence or naturally continuous multi-sentence statement in one speaker-visible shot if duration permits. **Do not fragment a single statement across successive panels merely because the storyboard text originally distributed its clauses across them.** Where the correct speaker remains visible in an existing shot, safely consolidate the whole statement there and make the next approved shot a silent reaction if appropriate. Preserve every word, the order of statements, emotional meaning, and the total clip duration.

Cross-cut dialogue is higher risk under this production's visible-mouth rule. Do not introduce it to solve a routine scheduling problem. If truly required by an approved script, request approval and follow the verified official `<scenetrans>`/continuity syntax; do not invent custom tags. If a line cannot fit naturally in a visible-speaker shot, escalate instead of splitting it arbitrarily.

## 10. Audio-source isolation — communications, screens, and recordings

**Key new safeguard from successful Scene 9 repair:** A headset, desk microphone, radio, intercom, telephone, television, video-call window, or computer monitor is a *visible prop*, not automatic permission for another voice. A command spoken toward a microphone is not automatically followed by an audible remote response.

For every such device, determine from the script whether it produces: (A) authorized audible speech, (B) authorized nonverbal device sounds, or (C) no sound. Record this decision in the manifest. If no remote dialogue is scripted, describe the device as visually present but with **no audible remote voice, intercom playback, recorded conversation, or background chatter**. Define a neutral local environmental sound bed independently of the device. Do not describe 'radio static with distant voices,' 'indistinct transmission,' 'muffled chatter,' or 'conversation through the headset' unless expressly authorized.

For a **silent video-only feed** or displayed person, establish that its audio channel is muted or inactive across the **entire relevant sequence**, including shots before the display is revealed. The person on the display must not speak or mouth words unless scripted. The display's activation may be visual only, or accompanied by a specifically authorized nonvocal sound.

For a scene with **authorized** remote dialogue, do not mute it indiscriminately: the approved speaker, timing, source, and on-screen/off-screen exception must be explicit. Because this production normally forbids off-screen voices, request approval when an audible remote speaker cannot be visibly shown speaking. Never remove a legitimate scripted reply to prevent hallucinations.

## 11. Silence is a positive sound design, not merely a prohibition

In fully silent (no-dialogue) clips, use **zero `<d>` tags and zero speaker IDs**. Specify concrete visual performance and only authorized physical ambience: room tone, ventilation, permitted electronics, distant traffic, fabric movement, etc., if established by the source. Avoid 'indistinct murmurs,' 'human activity in the distance,' 'crowd voices,' or 'faint speech' when no human voice is intended. Do not add unrequested beeps, vocal notifications, radio responses, or mood-setting sound effects.

In dialogue clips, each silent shot must explicitly say what the character *does* without speaking and what permitted sound continues. The same rule applies to silent intervals after a line. Use one concise global 'only tagged lines are spoken' instruction, with shot-specific audio-state definitions where needed; do not overwhelm the model with repeated escalating negative lists.

## 12. Voice performance aligned to visual acting

For each dialogue event, state the speaker's intended emotion, vocal intensity, pace, and delivery in concise natural language, consistent with facial expression, posture, and story context. A strained professional report should not sound like an angry shout; a furious command should sound forceful without acquiring new words. Specify relevant natural pauses or emphasis only when they fit the approved line and duration. Preserve the same speaker's voice identity and timbre in later shots. Voice directions are performance instructions, never separate speech events. Do not add gasps, grunts, laughter, sighs, or breathy utterances unless the screenplay authorizes them; ordinary subtle nonvocal breathing is not dialogue.

## 13. Storyboard full-frame reconstruction and first-frame lock

`<Picture 0>` is ALWAYS a multi-panel **shot-planning** reference, not an image to animate. Every panel maps to a separate full-screen 16:9 cinematic shot in reading order. The output must never show the whole contact sheet, panel borders, captions, thumbnails, multiple animated panels, a zoom into a panel, or the storyboard itself as an opening frame. Each hard cut completely replaces the previous camera view.

Integrate the exact mandatory storyboard and identity wording from Stylistic Preferences.txt, adapted to the actual asset map. Additionally describe the **specific physical scene content** of Panel 1 at the start of `detailed_description` and again briefly in `[Shot 1]`: at **00:00.000**, the first generated frame is **already** the full-screen live-action reconstruction of that composition, at the approved camera position. No preliminary grid, pre-roll, unrelated frame, fade from another angle, or camera move into the composition. The video begins *inside the fictional world*, not on a storyboard page.

Use positive, concrete opening geometry (e.g., where the seated figure, window, desk, lamp, and bed are located) rather than relying only on repeated 'do not show grid' instructions. A storyboard reference is NOT automatically a concrete first-frame image in official reference terminology. If the grid persists after one or two focused repairs, stop near-identical retries and propose user-approved clean individual-shot references or an editorial opening-shot workaround where supported. Do not promise that text alone guarantees frame-zero compliance.

## 14. Cinematic visual fidelity and hard-cut priority

Preserve the storyboard's exact camera direction, framing, spatial relationships, character visibility, prop continuity, and established light-source positions. Distinguish **camera direction** from a character's **eyeline**. Explicitly state the background behind the speaking face when that prevents a wrong-angle or wrong-person shot. Preserve dimensional facial lighting, natural skin, readable shadow-side eyes, motivated practical/window/monitor illumination, and controlled negative fill when compatible with the approved scene. Do not add lighting fixtures or move characters solely to create a prettier image.

Hard cuts occur at the optimized times regardless of whether incidental motion would otherwise continue. For action-heavy shots, begin with essential information already visually established where the approved panel permits; avoid lengthy setup that steals the next speaker's shot. Do not invent extra inserts, duplicates, camera moves, or altered panel coverage to rescue timing.

## 15. `overall_soundscape` and `non_diegetic_music` separation

`overall_soundscape` summarizes only the permitted environmental sound layers and the number/order/source of authorized dialogue events if useful. It must NOT repeat any scripted dialogue text, `<d>` tags, or invented speech. For a communications scene, explicitly separate quiet local room tone from any intentionally silent headset/monitor. Specify ambience-only intervals after the final line when material. Do not add speech-like 'murmurs' as a default ambience.

`non_diegetic_music` must use the user's mandatory rule: `None. Absolutely no background music, score, instrumental music, musical transitions, or music of any kind. Music will be added only in post-production.`

## 16. Mandatory pre-release audits (BLOCK on failure)

**Source and visual audit**
- Actual storyboard and original generation prompt inspected; authoritative dialogue, total duration, and reference map verified.
- Every panel mapped exactly once to a full-screen shot, in original order, with no invented or omitted composition.
- Opening frame at 00:00.000 is explicitly the full-screen physical Panel 1 scene; no contact sheet or animated storyboard.
- Correct identity from assigned turnaround; wardrobe, props, blocking, camera, lighting, and action continuity preserved.
- All cut times feasible and within the approved duration (maximum 15 seconds); no preceding action overruns a speaking shot.

**Dialogue manifest audit**
- Every scripted word and line present in exact chronological order; no substitutions, omissions, or additional words.
- Exactly one `<d>` tag per authorized dialogue event; zero tags for a silent clip; no duplicate quoted lines outside tags.
- Stable `(Sx)` by first vocal occurrence, correct `<Subject N>`, and visibly articulating correct speaker for every event.
- Each line begins after its face is established, fits naturally, and finishes before the next event/cut unless an explicitly approved exception applies.
- Continuous statements are not unnecessarily fragmented across shots.
- Each speaking shot specifies natural vocal release, resting mouth, and storyboard-grounded nonverbal follow-through.
- All silent gaps, final-line remainder, and fully silent shots have a concrete environmental-only or true-silence audio state.
- Devices, screens, and communications channels have an explicit permission state; no unintended remote voice or audio playback.
- Vocal direction matches visible acting; no invented breaths, filler, ad-libs, announcements, murmurs, or background dialogue.

**Format and delivery audit**
- Six official sections, correct labels, official shot timestamp format, valid `<d>[Language] ...</d>` tags, and correct reference order.
- `overall_soundscape` contains no duplicated line or dialogue tag; music is explicitly none.
- Prompt is detailed and standalone, not a terse summary or a wall of repetitive negative constraints.
- Before the prompt, provide a compact operator header and optimized shot schedule (shot, time range, visible speaker, exact line or SILENT, material reassignment).
- Deliver one complete copy/paste-ready prompt. If any blocking input or contradiction remains, ask or mark PROVISIONAL instead of falsely passing the audit.

## 17. Repair ladder — diagnose the observed failure, not the whole scene

**A. Repeated exact line:** Search for duplicated `<d>` tags or repeated quotation in soundscape/summary; keep one authoritative tag. Check post-line vocal release and final audio state.

**B. Improvised garbage after a correct line:** Confirm natural line completion, resting mouth, immediate environmental-only state, and prompt cut soon after the line when feasible. Remove invitations to continue speaking; do not merely add more 'no dialogue' repetitions.

**C. Phantom response during another character's shot:** Treat microphone/headset/intercom as a silent prop unless a remote line is scripted. Eliminate remote voice playback, radio chatter, and conversation framing. Establish the new visible speaker before the line.

**D. Wrong speaker or omitted line:** Rebuild manifest and shot schedule, verify stable IDs and correct visible mouth; keep lines close to their owner in each shot description. If necessary, move an intact event to an existing compliant shot.

**E. Continuous line split across panels:** Consolidate the entire statement into one speaker-visible shot with sufficient natural time; make the following approved panel a silent reaction if appropriate.

**F. Silent screen character speaks / voice begins before screen appears:** Declare the feed video-only and audio-muted across the relevant sequence, not only in the display shot. Remove vague human vocal ambience.

**G. Pre-first-line or between-line vocal filler:** Define environmental-only audio in the specific gap, keep the correct mouth at rest, and avoid prose implying an impending reply.

**H. Action overrun or missing speaker shot:** Pre-establish approved key information, reduce incidental setup, enforce hard cuts, and establish the speaking face at the new shot's start.

**I. Storyboard contact sheet or wrong opening frame:** State the exact Panel 1 full-screen physical composition at 00:00.000, not a generic prohibition alone; if persistent, request a different reference preparation workflow.

**J. Persistent failure after one or two targeted repairs:** Stop nearly identical reruns. Offer user-approved separate speaking-shot generations and editorial assembly, or a controlled external dialogue track where suitable. Neither guarantees success.

When repairing, preserve already successful lines, shots, visual assets, and duration unless a broader rebuild is necessary or requested. If the user requests a complete rebuild, reconstruct from the authoritative script, actual storyboard, and asset map rather than patching old prose.

## 19. Required conversion sequence (operational order)

1. Read all four reference files in the required order; report inaccessible files.
2. Verify storyboard, original generation prompt/script, total duration, and upload mapping.
3. Inspect actual panels and create shot inventory, including exact first-frame geometry and visible mouths.
4. Create exact dialogue manifest, stable speaker IDs, device-audio permissions, and silent-interval map.
5. Optimize shot timing and intact event placement only within permitted authority; escalate unresolved conflicts.
6. Draft six detailed MiniMax sections, with concrete frame-zero reconstruction, correct speaker-first dialogue, natural vocal release, and explicit authorized audio states.
7. Run all audits in Section 16. Repair deficiencies before delivery.
8. Deliver operator header, compact shot schedule, and one complete copyable prompt; log test results and use the focused repair ladder if generation fails.
