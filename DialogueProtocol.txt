# Dialogue Reliability Protocol — Supplementary Rules v1.0

**Purpose:** Reduce improvised, unintelligible, non-English, incorrectly attributed, or mistimed dialogue in MiniMax H3 video generation.

These rules supplement the Official MiniMax Prompting Guidelines and existing Stylistic Preferences. They do not supersede official syntax or authorize changes to the approved storyboard.

### 1. Exact-dialogue priority

For every video containing dialogue, construct a private dialogue manifest before drafting the prompt.

Record:
- Exact scripted words, copied verbatim.
- Speaking character and `<Subject N>` designation.
- Global `(Sx)` speaker ID, assigned by first vocal occurrence.
- Shot in which each line is spoken.
- Available speaking interval.
- Whether the dialogue continues across a cut.

Every spoken word in the final video must be traceable to an authorized dialogue event.

### 2. Dialogue-first shot construction

Within each speaking shot in `detailed_description`:

1. Establish the camera composition and visible speaking subject.
2. Identify the speaker using `<Subject N> (Sx)`.
3. Specify the intended vocal delivery concisely.
4. Place the exact scripted words inside `<d>[English] ...</d>`.
5. Describe the necessary synchronized facial performance and subsequent action.

Do not bury the dialogue beneath long passages of secondary visual instructions.

### 3. Spoken-language enforcement

For English dialogue, explicitly require natural, intelligible spoken English.

The character must articulate the exact scripted words in the specified order, without paraphrasing, substituted words, invented syllables, additional phrases, or unintelligible vocalizations.

Do not invent phonetic spellings, change the script's language, or introduce an accent not specified by the source.

### 4. Speaker ownership

Every speaking event must pair the visible subject with the correct speaker ID.

Use `<Subject N> (Sx)` consistently at the actual vocal event. Never assume subject numbers and speaker numbers are identical.

Do not allow a character to deliver another character's dialogue.

In telephone conversations, each character speaks only when the approved shot plan assigns dialogue to that character. Preserve any explicitly scripted off-screen speech, but do not invent remote telephone voices.

### 5. Dialogue crossing shot boundaries

When a single sentence continues across a cut, treat it as one continuous vocal performance.

Maintain the same speaker ID, vocal timbre, language, pace, and emotional delivery.

Use the official MiniMax dialogue-continuity notation, including `<scenetrans>` where appropriate.

Do not turn a sentence fragment into a separate, unrelated speech event.

Do not alter the approved shot boundary or redistribute words without authorization.

### 6. Dialogue timing feasibility

Before finalizing the prompt, check whether the scripted words can be spoken naturally within the allocated shot duration.

Allow adequate time for intelligible pronunciation, necessary breaths, and scripted emotional pauses.

Avoid unnecessary subsecond instructions that fragment ordinary conversational speech.

If a line cannot reasonably fit, flag the conflict for user approval. Never silently shorten, accelerate, paraphrase, or relocate the dialogue.

### 7. Silent-shot discipline

For shots without authorized dialogue, explicitly state that the visible characters remain silent.

Preserve intended listening, facial reactions, and environmental ambience.

Do not generate filler words, muttering, invented telephone speech, or unscripted vocal reactions.

Keep silence instructions concise rather than repeating extensive negative lists throughout the shot.

### 8. Prompt clarity and instruction density

Preserve the complete approved visual plan, cinematic detail, character identity, and environmental continuity.

However, remove redundant dialogue-related instructions that repeat the same prohibition many times.

Keep detailed visual directions and exact dialogue instructions clearly distinguishable.

Use one concise global dialogue restriction, supplemented by specific speaker assignments within the relevant shots.

### 9. Audio-section separation

Place exact spoken dialogue only inside `detailed_description`, using official `<d>` notation.

Use `overall_soundscape` for the authorized environmental ambience and sound effects. Do not duplicate complete dialogue lines there.

Retain the existing prohibition against non-diegetic music.

### 10. Mandatory dialogue-reliability audit

Before releasing each prompt, verify:

- Every scripted word is preserved exactly.
- Every dialogue event has the correct speaker ID.
- The speaking character is correct for the shot.
- Every English line uses valid `<d>[English] ...</d>` syntax.
- Speech crossing a cut uses appropriate official continuity notation.
- The spoken line fits the available time naturally.
- Silent shots contain no unintended dialogue events.
- No contradictory vocal instructions appear elsewhere.
- No additional dialogue has been invented.
- No approved shot, camera composition, duration, or dramatic action has been changed.

**Release rule:** A prompt must pass both the existing visual-continuity audit and this dialogue-reliability audit before delivery.

**Important limitation:** These rules reduce avoidable prompting ambiguity but cannot guarantee deterministic speech synthesis. If gibberish persists despite a compliant prompt, report the failure and consider a user-approved change to dialogue segmentation or an external dialogue-audio workflow.
