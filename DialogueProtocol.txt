# Dialogue Reliability Protocol — Supplementary Rules v1.3

**Purpose:** Govern direct conversion from an approved storyboard image and its original storyboard-generation prompt into a MiniMax H3 full-reference prompt, while reducing improvised, unintelligible, non-English, incorrectly attributed, repeated, out-of-order, or mistimed dialogue in MiniMax H3 full-reference video generation.

**Status:** Revised from v1.2 to adopt direct storyboard-to-MiniMax conversion and controlled dialogue/timing optimization. Supplements official MiniMax syntax and the minimally updated Stylistic Preferences v1.1. The storyboard remains authoritative for visual coverage; this protocol controls the permitted dialogue and timing decisions. Prompting controls are not guarantees of deterministic generation.

## 1. Exact-dialogue priority and private manifest

Before drafting, construct a private chronological dialogue manifest recording: (a) exact verbatim scripted words and punctuation, (b) speaking character and stable `<Subject N>`, (c) global `(Sx)` speaker ID assigned by first vocal occurrence, (d) assigned shot and storyboard panel, (e) start/end of the shot and feasible speaking interval, (f) whether the speaker is visibly speaking, (g) whether speech crosses a cut, and (h) the moment after which no further speech is authorized. Include intentional silent shots. Record whether the shot begins with the required visual state already established and whether a preceding physical action could overrun its cut. Every spoken word must be traceable to an authorized event. Never rewrite, shorten, paraphrase, reorder, or add scripted words. Redistribution of complete authorized dialogue events between existing storyboard shots is permitted only under Sections 13–16, with the same speaker, exact wording, narrative order, and visible-mouth requirements.

## 2. Dialogue-first shot construction

In each speaking shot of `detailed_description`, establish the approved full-screen composition and clearly visible speaking subject BEFORE speech begins, then identify `<Subject N> (Sx)`, give one concise vocal direction, insert the exact line once in `<d>[English] ...</d>`, and describe synchronized lips plus the post-line visual action. Keep the dialogue near the beginning of the shot description rather than burying it beneath long secondary directions. Preserve detailed cinematography, lighting, blocking, and acting, but separate these instructions from the dialogue event. Do not duplicate the full scripted line in `summary`, `overall_soundscape`, global restrictions, or other shots.

## 3. Spoken-language enforcement

Require natural, intelligible English for English dialogue. Preserve every word in script order, without paraphrase, substitutions, invented syllables, additional phrases, or non-English improvisation. Never invent phonetic spellings or accents. Each `<d>` tag is an exact authorized speech event, not a suggestion to improvise around the line.

## 4. Speaker ownership and visual proof

Use the same `<Subject N>` consistently and assign `(S1)`, `(S2)`, etc. by first vocal occurrence in the target video, not by subject numbering. At each vocal event, explicitly associate the speaker ID with the visible speaking character. Prefer a clear view of that character's face and mouth during the line when the approved storyboard permits it. Other visible characters are listeners and do not articulate speech. Do not assume that a character shown in the previous or next shot owns the current line. When successive shots change speakers, make the cut to the new visible speaking face an explicit prerequisite to the new line; never let the preceding face carry the next speaker's dialogue. A dialogue shot may contain multiple characters if the speaking character is unambiguous. Do not change the approved camera composition, shot count, shot order, or visible characters without approval. Reassignment of dialogue to another existing, speaker-visible shot is permitted under Sections 13–16.

**Production-specific restriction:** For this user's AI filmmaking workflow, no voiceover or off-screen dialogue. Every authorized line must be delivered by its actual speaker visibly speaking at that moment. If a script or existing shot plan requires off-screen speech, flag the conflict for user decision rather than silently changing it. This production restriction is stricter than the generic telephone/off-screen allowance in v1.0.

## 5. Dialogue crossing shot boundaries

Prefer one complete sentence or naturally indivisible dialogue event per speaker-visible shot. The converter may adjust shot timing and reassign an intact event to another existing shot under Sections 13–16, but may not change camera compositions, shot order, speaker, or wording. If a scripted line must cross a cut, preserve one continuous vocal event, stable speaker ID, voice timbre, pace, and emotion; use official MiniMax continuity notation, including `<scenetrans>` where applicable and verified. Do not split a sentence across shots merely to fit an inherited schedule. Do not change the word order or move individual words independently. If a cross-cut line is unavoidable, request approval for that higher-risk exception. If a cut makes visible-speaker ownership impossible under the no-off-screen-dialogue rule, flag it before conversion.

## 6. Dialogue timing and chronological lock

Check that every line fits naturally in its assigned shot, including pronunciation and intended dramatic pauses. Record a chronological speaking schedule before writing. Where helpful, specify the line's approximate completion time **before** the next hard cut; avoid excessive subsecond micromanagement of ordinary speech. Each complete event finishes before the next event starts unless the screenplay expressly authorizes overlap or cross-cut continuity. Never permit the model to infer additional dialogue from unfilled shot time. Protect hard-cut boundaries from action overrun: when a reveal or prop manipulation precedes dialogue, start with the important visual information already visible where the approved composition permits, minimize nonessential manipulation, and state that the cut occurs on schedule even if incidental movement has not completed. Reserve sufficient screen time for every speaking face, especially when the speaker changes. First optimize existing shot durations and dialogue assignment under Sections 13–16. If the dialogue still cannot fit naturally within the approved total duration and visible-speaker coverage, stop and request approval for a storyboard revision, longer clip, or separate generation. Never rush speech to force a fit.

## 7. Silent-shot discipline and final dialogue cutoff

For a silent shot, specify a concrete nonverbal performance: gaze, brow, posture, hands, and quiet reaction, grounded in the storyboard. Explicitly state that the characters do not speak. Define environmental-only audio for that shot; do not describe an anticipated verbal reply, an unspoken question about to be voiced, or an invitation to continue talking.

**Final-line rule (new):** Identify the last authorized dialogue event. Specify its natural completion point and state that from that moment to the end of the video, the soundtrack contains only authorized environmental sounds. When a silent reaction follows the last line, make that shot explicitly nonverbal. Avoid placing new speech-like cues after the final `<d>` tag, including repeated quotation or a speculative conversational response. After the final vocal release, specify a natural closed/resting mouth and a concrete silent gaze or posture; cut promptly to a nonverbal reaction shot if the approved edit calls for one. State the environmental-only remainder once in the shot and once in `overall_soundscape` at most, rather than repeating long prohibition lists. Do not impose a completion time that would make the line unnaturally rushed.

## 8. Prompt clarity, density, and storyboard fidelity

Keep all six required MiniMax sections and preserve the approved visual plan. Use one clear chronological dialogue sequence in `detailed_description`; put each complete scripted line in one dialogue tag only. Avoid repeating exact dialogue in headings, summaries, soundscape, or constraints. Avoid long lists of vocal prohibitions after each line; use one concise global restriction and one specific silent-shot statement where needed. The storyboard remains a planning reference, not a visible grid. Reproduce each panel as a separate full-screen cinematic shot. Camera direction and eyeline are different: describe the camera's viewing direction, the background behind the speaker, and who must remain outside the frame where necessary. This framing control addresses a separate visual failure observed during Scene 2; it is not a dialogue syntax requirement.

## 9. Audio-section separation

Place exact scripted words only inside `<d>` tags in `detailed_description`. `overall_soundscape` describes authorized room tone, environmental effects, and, if useful, the number/order of speech events **without repeating their words**. If the final dialogue has ended, state the ambience-only interval here once. `non_diegetic_music` remains none unless the user expressly authorizes otherwise. Do not add improvised human utterances as ambience.

## 10. Mandatory dialogue-reliability audit

Before delivery verify:

- Each mandatory hard cut occurs before the next speaker begins; no lengthy preceding action can consume the next shot's speaking interval.
- The opening visual state of an action-heavy shot minimizes avoidable setup without changing the approved storyboard meaning.
- Each speaking face is established on screen before its dialogue starts, especially at speaker changes.

- The dialogue manifest matches the script word-for-word and in order.
- Every speaking event has one correct stable `<Subject N>` and global `(Sx)` ID.
- The correct speaking face is visibly present during each line, consistent with the storyboard and production's no-off-screen-speech rule.
- Every English line uses valid `<d>[English] ...</d>` notation and appears exactly once.
- Each line has a feasible interval and ends before the next event unless approved continuity notation applies.
- The final spoken event has an explicit, natural termination point; subsequent shots are silent and ambience-only where required.
- Non-speaking characters have no speech cues or lip articulation instructions.
- No duplicate dialogue text appears in `summary`, `overall_soundscape`, or repeated global instructions.
- All approved shot framing, camera positions, shot count/order, character identities, acting intent, and visual continuity remain intact; cut times and dialogue-to-shot assignments may change only within the controlled discretion in Sections 13–16.
- All six MiniMax sections are present and reference assignments follow the actual upload order (`<Picture 0>` storyboard, subsequent pictures per explicit asset map).

**Release rule:** Pass both the visual-continuity audit and this dialogue-reliability audit before delivering a complete standalone prompt.

## 11. Failure diagnosis and repair ladder (new)

**A. Wrong speaker / duplicated line:** Verify the actual speaker-to-subject mapping, global ID order, visible mouth, and shot boundary. Repair only affected events; preserve already successful shots.

**B. Dialogue out of order:** Simplify to a single chronological dialogue sequence, one `<d>` tag per authorized event, with one visible speaker per speaking shot when already compatible with the storyboard. Remove redundant vocal instructions. Do not change shots or script. Use the permitted dialogue/timing optimization before escalating for approval.

**C. Improvised or unintelligible dialogue between lines:** Keep visual detail but shorten speech directions, eliminate repeated dialogue text, and explicitly define each event's completion before the next shot.

**D. Garbage speech after the last line (Scene 3 Video 12, observed successful repair):** Keep authorized speech unchanged unless the user separately approves a screenplay correction. Give the last line a feasible completion point and natural vocal release; close the speaking mouth, cut to the scheduled silent wide reaction, and define an ambience-only remainder. Reduce redundant speech prohibitions and speech-like cues after the last dialogue tag. The corrected regeneration succeeded, but the specific causal contribution of each adjustment is unproven.

**E. Action overrun / missing speaker shot (Scene 3 Video 09, observed successful repair):** Diagnose whether a prolonged reveal or prop action delays a hard cut. Pre-expose the key visual information at frame one where compatible with the storyboard; reduce secondary motion; lock the cut before the next speech; establish the new speaker's face before that line. Preserve all approved panels and dialogue. In the successful 12-second regeneration, the tattoo was already visible, the first shot was silent and short, and the speaking shots were explicitly allocated to ALEX, BEN, ALEX. The combined intervention succeeded; the individual causal contribution of each change remains unproven.

**F. Incorrect framing / unwanted background character:** Describe the storyboard's camera geometry, screen position, and background exclusions precisely. Do not assume an eyeline establishes camera placement. Preserve shots already rendering correctly.

**G. Storyboard rendered as an animated grid:** Retain mandatory storyboard-as-planning-only wording from Stylistic Preferences. If it persists, request user approval to test clean borderless per-shot references where the workflow supports them.

**H. Persistent failure after targeted prompt repair:** Stop near-identical prompt retries. Offer user-approved separate speaking-shot generations and editorial assembly, or a controlled external dialogue-audio workflow. Do not claim either workaround guarantees success.

## 12. Evidence and change control (new)

Track the clip number, version, failure category, changed prompt sections, and observed output. Preserve successful lines, shots, and reference assignments when repairing a different failure. Classify findings as (1) directly observed improvements, (2) plausible but unproven mechanisms, or (3) untested fallback options. In Scene 2, the simplified chronological structure coincided with correct speaker order and dialogue sequencing; a later final-line cutoff repair was followed by the user's report that all clips generated without problems. These observations support testing the new rules but do not isolate causal effects or guarantee future success.

## 13. Direct storyboard-to-MiniMax workflow (v1.3)

**Default input:** (1) the completed, user-approved multi-panel storyboard image; (2) the original storyboard-generation prompt containing the exact dialogue, action, emotional intention, and any duration information; (3) the explicit character/prop/environment reference images and their upload mapping; and (4) the target clip duration or approved maximum. A Seedance 2.0 video-generation prompt is **not required** and must not be requested as a routine prerequisite. If the storyboard-generation prompt omits exact dialogue or materially conflicts with the image, request the missing authoritative screenplay excerpt or a targeted clarification; never reconstruct spoken words from guesswork. An optional Seedance prompt, if supplied, is secondary contextual material, not the governing timing schedule.

**Source authority:** The completed storyboard controls shot count, shot order, camera viewpoint, framing, visible characters, blocking, spatial continuity, environment, lighting, and visual acting. Assigned character reference images control identity and wardrobe. The original storyboard-generation prompt controls scripted words, speaker identity, narrative action, emotional delivery, and intended sound where compatible with the image. Official MiniMax guidelines control syntax. This Dialogue Protocol controls *permitted* timing and dialogue placement. Where a visual/dialogue contradiction cannot be resolved within that authority, stop for user approval.

**Reference order:** `<Picture 0>` storyboard; `<Picture 1>` first explicitly assigned reference; `<Picture 2>` second explicitly assigned reference; further images as explicitly mapped. Do not assume `<Subject N>` equals `<Picture N>`. Retain the six-section full-reference format and a complete standalone prompt.

## 14. Controlled timing and dialogue-placement authority (v1.3)

The converter is expressly authorized, **without seeking approval for each ordinary adjustment**, to:

1. Reallocate the durations of existing storyboard shots, including shortening or lengthening silent reactions, while preserving the approved **total clip duration** and every shot's existence and chronological order.
2. Move an **entire, verbatim sentence or indivisible dialogue event** to a different existing storyboard shot in which the *correct* speaker's face and articulating mouth are clearly visible, provided that the original dialogue order, causal sequence, emotional meaning, and continuity remain intact.
3. Choose feasible dialogue onset, completion, breath, and pause positions within those shots; adjust the relative time assigned to incidental actions where the storyboard permits, without deleting required actions or changing their causal order.
4. Convert a shot to an expressly silent reaction when its original dialogue is safely reassigned elsewhere, preserving the depicted performance and story meaning.

The converter is **not** authorized to change the words, speaker, sentence order, shot count, shot order, camera angle, framing, shot contents, blocking, essential action, wardrobe, identity, location, or total duration; invent inserts, reverse angles, duplicates, dialogue, or new visual beats; or exceed the MiniMax 15-second limit. It must not turn a back-facing or obscured listener shot into a speaking shot simply because the shot has available time. A partial mouth in profile is acceptable only if clearly visible and the identity is unambiguous. Do not rotate a head, move the camera, or add an insert to manufacture mouth visibility.

**Default priority:** exact dialogue and speaker → visible speaking mouth → original narrative sequence → complete lines within shots → feasible performance timing → preservation of every approved visual panel. If those requirements conflict, do not quietly sacrifice one; escalate.

## 15. Mandatory pre-conversion feasibility and scheduling audit (v1.3)

Before drafting, inspect **each actual storyboard panel**, not merely the intended text description. Create a working shot inventory recording panel number, visible people, camera-facing versus back-facing faces, mouth visibility, action, emotional beat, camera direction, and possible dialogue ownership. Compare it to the original storyboard-generation prompt and exact dialogue manifest.

Build an **optimized chronological shot-and-dialogue schedule** before writing the six-section prompt. For each shot specify start/end time, assigned dialogue event or `SILENT`, speaker subject/ID, visibility, and expected vocal completion. Allocate enough time for natural emotional delivery, articulation, breathing, and shot transition; avoid excessively precise forced phoneme timing. Reserve meaningful time for all approved panels. Protect hard cuts from long preliminary actions; no earlier shot may consume a later shot's allocation. When possible, finish each line comfortably before its cut, with a natural vocal release and resting mouth.

A single sentence should normally stay within one shot. A reaction close-up of BEN must not be made to speak ALEX's line; an over-the-shoulder view of a speaker's back does not satisfy visible-speaker proof. If no existing shot provides feasible coverage for an authorized line, **block prompt release** and request a targeted storyboard/timing decision. Do not invent a new camera angle to solve it.

**Operator output:** Before the copy/paste-ready MiniMax prompt, show a compact table with shot, optimized time range, visible speaker, exact line (or silent), and any material deviation from the storyboard-generation prompt's suggested schedule. This schedule is a transparent operator aid, not additional MiniMax syntax. Do not demand a separate approval for compliant optimizations unless the user explicitly requests approval checkpoints.

**Persistent failures:** After one or two targeted revisions without improvement, stop expanding repetitive negative instructions. Offer a user-approved fallback: generate the speaking shot separately and assemble the approved silent shots editorially, or use a controlled post-production dialogue track if compatible with the user's workflow. Do not claim that longer prompts or stronger prohibitions guarantee compliance.

**Evidence standard:** Record the original failure, changed schedule, prompt version, actual regeneration result, and whether success was user-confirmed. Treat the Scene 5 repairs above as *tested hypotheses only where an outcome was reported*; do not mark them successful without confirmation. Preserve existing Scene 3 success records in Section 12.

## 17. Final direct-conversion release checklist (v1.3)

- [ ] Completed storyboard image and original storyboard-generation prompt both examined; exact script dialogue available.
- [ ] Reference map is explicit, with `<Picture 0>` storyboard and no assumed subject/picture-number correspondence.
- [ ] Every panel appears exactly once, in original order, as a full-screen shot; no invented camera setup or duplicate person.
- [ ] Shot durations optimized within the approved total and maximum 15 seconds; no shot starved or skipped.
- [ ] Every complete dialogue event belongs to the correct visible speaking face, in exact script order and wording, with natural timing.
- [ ] No dialogue is assigned to a back-facing/occluded speaker or to a listener's reaction shot.
- [ ] Silent shots have concrete nonverbal performance and environmental-only audio; final-line cutoff is explicit.
- [ ] One `<d>` tag per authorized vocal event; stable `<Subject N>` and `(Sx)` mapping; no redundant exact dialogue elsewhere.
- [ ] All six official MiniMax sections present, with detailed shot-by-shot visual descriptions, no music, and no added speech.
- [ ] Operator header and optimized shot/dialogue schedule supplied; any unresolvable conflict is flagged instead of guessed.
